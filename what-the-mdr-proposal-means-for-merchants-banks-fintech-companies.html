What is the MDR Proposal? Understanding Its Impact on India's Digital Payment Ecosystem

Published on Aug 07, 2026 • by SecureEdge Team

India has become one of the largest markets in the world for digital payments. Millions of people use digital payment apps every day to pay bills, make online payments, transfer money, and buy local goods and Services. Digital payments have revolutionized the financial system of the country by making transactions faster, safer and more convenient. Recently, the Government of India introduced a proposal through the Taxation and Other Laws Amendment Bill (2026) that reinstated the Merchant Discount Rate. The proposal doesn't introduce MDR on digital payments immediately but it gives the government the authority to announce MDR at a later date for certain digital payment methods. This proposal could influence the future of digital payments and payment services for banks, fintechs and merchants. This blog will explain what the MDR proposal is, how it impacts stakeholders and how business can prepare.

Understanding Merchant Discount Rate (MDR)

Merchant Discount Rate is the transaction fee merchants pay when they accept digital payment through payment gateways or other electronic payment methods. When a customer pays digitally, the merchant gets the amount of the payment after deducting the MDR, which is a small percentage.

For example:
  • Customer purchases goods worth ₹10,000
  • Applicable MDR = 0.30%
  • Merchant receives ₹9,970
  • ₹30 is shared among the acquiring bank, issuing bank, payment service provider, and payment network

This fee helps cover the cost of maintaining payment infrastructure, fraud prevention systems, settlement processes, cybersecurity and transaction processing.

Why Was MDR Removed Earlier?

To accelerate digital payment adoption across India, the government introduced a Zero MDR policy for certain digital payment methods, including many UPI merchant transactions.

The objective was straightforward:
  • Encourage merchants to accept digital payments
  • Promote financial inclusion
  • Reduce cash dependency
  • Increase digital transaction volumes
  • Support India's Digital India initiative

The strategy worked exceptionally well. India now processes billions of digital transactions every month, making its payment ecosystem one of the fastest-growing in the world. However, payment service providers, banks, and fintech companies have consistently argued that maintaining payment infrastructure without a sustainable revenue model presents long-term challenges.

What Does the New MDR Proposal Actually Mean?

One of the biggest misconceptions is that the government has already imposed MDR on UPI payments.

That is not correct: The proposal simply removes the legal restriction that prevented the government from introducing MDR on certain notified digital payment systems.

In simple words:
  • No new MDR has been implemented yet.
  • Customer-to-customer (P2P) transactions are expected to remain free.
  • Future MDR, if introduced, may apply only to selected merchant payments.
  • The government will have the flexibility to notify specific payment categories if required.

This provides policymakers with greater flexibility to create a sustainable financial model for the digital payments ecosystem while balancing merchant interests and consumer convenience.

Why Is This Proposal Important?

India's digital payment infrastructure has grown rapidly over the past decade. Maintaining this ecosystem requires continuous investment in:

  • Cybersecurity
  • Fraud monitoring
  • API infrastructure
  • Cloud platforms
  • Banking integrations
  • Customer support
  • Regulatory compliance
  • Technology upgrades

Banks and fintech companies invest heavily in ensuring that every transaction is secure, reliable, and processed within seconds. A sustainable revenue mechanism could encourage greater innovation, stronger security, and continued investment in payment infrastructure.

Impact on Merchants

Merchants are likely to be the most closely affected stakeholders if MDR is introduced in the future. Small businesses have significantly benefited from zero-MDR digital payments because they receive the complete transaction amount without paying additional processing charges. If MDR is introduced for specific merchant transactions, businesses may need to evaluate how transaction costs affect their operations. Possible impacts include:

Better Payment Infrastructure

Revenue generated through MDR could encourage payment companies to improve infrastructure, reducing payment failures and improving transaction speed.

Increased Access to Advanced Payment Solutions

Banks and fintech companies may introduce better merchant dashboards, analytics, fraud detection tools, automated reconciliation, and loyalty programs.

Cost Considerations

Large merchants processing high transaction volumes may need to factor MDR into their operating expenses. However, policymakers may continue offering exemptions or lower charges for small merchants.

Impact on Banks

Banks play a central role in India's digital payment ecosystem. They manage customer accounts, process transactions, ensure settlement between institutions, and maintain regulatory compliance. If MDR becomes applicable to selected merchant transactions, banks could benefit from:

  • Sustainable revenue generation
  • Higher investment in digital banking infrastructure
  • Better fraud prevention systems
  • Improved customer experience
  • Enhanced payment security
  • Expansion of digital banking services

Banks could also accelerate investments in AI-driven fraud detection, real-time payment monitoring, and advanced identity verification technologies.

Impact on FinTech Companies

For fintech companies, the MDR proposal could be more than a revenue discussion—it could influence how future payment ecosystems are designed.

Many fintech businesses have invested heavily in:
  • Digital onboarding
  • Identity verification
  • Payment APIs
  • Banking integrations
  • Merchant platforms
  • Risk assessment
  • Fraud prevention
  • Compliance automation

A sustainable payment ecosystem enables fintech companies to continue building innovative solutions while improving customer experience and transaction security. Companies offering Identity Verification APIs , Bank Account Verification APIs, Document OCR, Business Verification APIs, and Digital Customer Onboarding solutions may also see growing demand as financial institutions continue strengthening digital infrastructure.

Why the MDR Proposal Matters for India's Digital Payment Future

India has created one of the world's largest real-time payments ecosystems. Digital payments are now an integral part of the daily operations of millions of businesses. Payment ecosystems that are secure, scalable and financially viable will be more in demand as transaction volumes increase. The MDR proposal does not just involve introducing a fee for transactions. The MDR proposal is not simply about introducing a transaction fee.

A sustainable ecosystem can help:
  • Strengthen payment security.
  • Reduce transaction failures.
  • Support innovation in payment technologies.
  • Improve fraud prevention capabilities.
  • Deliver a better payment experience for merchants and customers.

Challenges Businesses Should Be Aware Of

While the proposal presents opportunities, businesses should also understand the possible challenges if MDR is introduced in the future.

1. Additional Operating Costs

Businesses with high daily transaction volumes may experience an increase in payment processing expenses. Companies should review their payment strategies and operational budgets accordingly.

2. Customer Pricing Decisions

Some merchants may choose to absorb the additional cost, while others could adjust product pricing. Any such decisions should prioritize transparency and customer trust.

3. Technology Upgrades

Banks and payment service providers may introduce new settlement procedures, reporting tools and merchant services. Businesses must stay informed to ensure that their payment systems are compliant with any future regulations.

Opportunities for Banks and FinTech Companies

For banks and fintech companies, the proposal could encourage greater investment in technologies that improve security, efficiency, and customer experience.

Key areas of innovation include:
  • AI-powered fraud detection
  • Real-time payment monitoring
  • Digital KYC solutions
  • Identity Verification APIs
  • Document OCR
  • Business Verification APIs
  • Automated compliance monitoring
  • Secure digital onboarding
  • API-first banking platforms

As digital payments continue to evolve, organizations that invest in scalable and secure technology will be better positioned to serve merchants and consumers.

What Should Merchants Do Now?

Although no new MDR has been implemented yet, merchants should stay informed and prepare for potential policy changes.

Some practical steps include:
  • Monitor official announcements from the Government of India, RBI, and NPCI.
  • Review your current payment acceptance strategy.
  • Compare payment providers and settlement options.
  • Invest in secure digital payment infrastructure.
  • Strengthen customer verification processes.
  • Work with trusted fintech partners that prioritize compliance and security.

Being proactive today can help businesses adapt more easily if future regulations evolve.

Final Thoughts

India's digital payment journey is one of the most impressive success stories on the global fintech scene. The MDR proposal is not just a discussion on transaction fees. It is an opportunity to create a digital payment ecosystem that is more innovative, resilient and sustainable. It is not clear whether MDR will be introduced in the future for specific payment categories. However, it is certain that digital payments will continue their growth, and businesses who invest in secure infrastructures, compliance and customer service will have a better chance of long-term success. The key for merchants is to be informed and strategic in their adaptation. It is an opportunity for banks to improve their payment infrastructure. Fintech companies are reminded of the importance innovation, secure APIs and seamless digital onboarding. India's financial system continues to develop. Collaboration between regulators and banks, as well as merchants and fintech firms, will be essential in ensuring digital payments are accessible, secure and sustainable.
