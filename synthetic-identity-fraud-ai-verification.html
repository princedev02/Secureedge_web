Synthetic Identity Fraud: How Criminals are Using AI To Beat Verification (And Businesses Can Stay Ahead).

Published on july 24, 2026 • by SecureEdge Team

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is changing industries at a rapid pace. Cybercriminals use the same technology as businesses to develop more sophisticated fraud schemes. Synthetic Identity Fraud is one of today's fastest-growing threats. Synthetic identities, unlike traditional identity thefts, are not completely stolen. They are carefully created using a mixture of real and false information. Criminals can now create identities that bypass outdated verification methods thanks to the growth of deepfakes and generative AI. Understanding Synthetic Identity Fraud has become a necessity for all businesses that rely on digital onboarding. This includes banks, fintechs, NBFCs, and insurance companies. Implementing robust Identity Verification APIs is one of the most effective ways to reduce onboarding fraud - especially where deepfakes are now targeting video KYC directly, a threat we cover in more depth in our guide to deepfake fraud in video KYC.

What is Synthetic Identity Fraud (SIF)?

Synthetic identity fraud is widely recognized as one of the fastest-growing financial crime patterns globally, and India's rapidly digitizing lending and onboarding ecosystem is no exception.

A fraudster, for example, may use:
  • The real PAN number
  • Fake name
  • A face generated by AI
  • Temporary mobile number
  • Fake address
  • AI-generated supporting documentation

These details, taken individually, may seem genuine. Together, these details create an identity which does not belong to a real person. However, it can pass basic verification checks.Synthetic identity fraud is much harder to detect than identity theft.

Why is it called "Synthetic identity fraud 2.0" ?

Artificial Intelligence is the latest fraud wave. Criminals used to rely on documents that were manually edited and credentials stolen. AI allows them to automate fraud and scale it using digital assets that are highly realistic.

Modern fraud techniques include:
  • AI-generated profile photos
  • Fake selfies to verify facial recognition
  • Voice cloning to improve customer service interactions
  • AI-created utility bills and bank statements
  • Fake employment records
  • Synthetic social media profiles
  • Automated chatbot interactions during onboarding

The AI is becoming more accessible and it requires less technical knowledge to create convincing fake identities.

How criminals use AI to beat verification

Fraud has become more sophisticated and convincing.

1. Create a Digital Identity

AI image generators can create human faces that never existed

2. Generating supporting documents

The fraudsters are using AI-powered tools that generate fake documents such as identity documents, proofs of address, salary slips and financial records.

3. Passing Face Verification

Deepfake technologies can be used to manipulate videos or selfies in order to bypass systems lacking advanced liveness detection.

4. Building Trust Over Time

Criminals do not use fake identities instantly, but instead build up credit histories, transaction records and digital footprints over time before they commit large-scale fraud.

5. Exploiting Weak Verification Systems

AI attacks are more likely to target organizations that rely solely on document uploads and one-time validation.

Industries most at Risk

Synthetic Identity Fraud affects more than just banks.

Industries at risk include:
  • Banking and Financial Services
  • Fintech Companies
  • Digital Lending Platforms
  • Insurance Providers
  • Stockbroking Platforms
  • Crypto Exchanges
  • E-commerce Marketplaces
  • Telecom Operators
  • Companies That Provide HR Services
  • Gig Economy Platforms

Targets can be any organization that offers online account creation, onboarding digitally, or financial transactions.

Why traditional verification is no longer enough

Many businesses still use verification methods that were designed to combat older fraud techniques.

Some of the most common limitations include:
  • Manual document reviews
  • OCR without authentication checks
  • One-time identity verification
  • Basic selfie matching
  • Password-based authentication
  • Limited fraud intelligence

These methods are unable to detect AI-generated documents, or synthetic digital identity. Modern fraud requires modern verification. Businesses need layers of verification that combine document validation, identification verification, biometrics checks, device intelligence and behavioral analysis.

Synthetic Identity Fraud: Warning Signs

Despite the fact that synthetic identities are increasingly plausible, certain patterns can indicate a high risk.

Look out for:
  • Multiple accounts created from the same device
  • Recently activated phone numbers
  • Disposable email addresses
  • AI-generated profile images
  • Inconsistent geolocation and address information
  • Inconsistent identification information across documents
  • Repeated verification attempts
  • Unusual onboarding behavior
  • Suspicious transaction patterns shortly after onboarding

Early detection of these fraud indicators can reduce losses by a significant amount.

How businesses can protect themselves

A single method of verification cannot stop sophisticated fraud. The best defense is a multi-layered approach. Businesses should consider:

Multi-layer Identity Verification

Verify your customers by using several trusted data sources, rather than just a single document.

Document Verification

Validate document authenticity and metadata.

PAN Verification

Verify that the customer's identity matches government records prior to onboarding.

Aadhaar Verification

Wherever possible, use digital methods of verification that are secure and in compliance with the regulations.

Bank Account Verification

Verify account ownership prior to processing payments or disbursing money.

Biometric Liveness Detection

Distinguish between real people and AI-generated videos, images or deepfakes.

Device Intelligence

Monitor suspected devices, emulator use, and repeated attempts at onboarding.

Risk-based Decision Making

Use historical data, behavioral patterns and verification results to assign risk scores. The combination of these layers provides a stronger defense against fraud. .

Why continuous verification is important

Fraud prevention should not stop with customer onboarding. Cybercriminals are constantly adapting their methods. Businesses should monitor the behavior of customers throughout their relationship and not just at registration.

Continuous verification helps detect:
  • Account takeover
  • Suspicious transactions
  • Credential misuse
  • Identity changes
  • Unusual login patterns

An active approach reduces financial losses and improves customer trust. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) also emphasizes stronger digital risk management practices for regulated entities.

Modern Identity Verification: Best Practices

Organisations that want to improve fraud prevention should concentrate on:

  • Implementing API-based verification workflows
  • Real-time identity validation
  • Regularly updating fraud detection models
  • Monitoring high-risk transactions
  • Educating employees about AI fraud
  • Conducting regular security assessments
  • Integrating multiple verification checkpoints throughout the customer journey

Businesses that combine technology and strong operational processes will be better able to combat fraud.

Identity Verification: The Future

AI will continue to shape fraud and fraud prevention. Businesses must invest in systems that can identify suspicious behavior in real-time as criminals develop more sophisticated techniques. Identity verification is more than just confirming documents. It is also about building digital trust by implementing advanced analytics and adaptive risk management. Modernizing verification processes will help organizations protect their customers, ensure regulatory compliance and reduce fraud losses.

Final Thoughts

Synthetic identity fraud is one of the biggest threats to digital businesses. Traditional verification methods are becoming increasingly ineffective due to the combination of AI-generated identity, deepfakes and sophisticated automation. It's good to know that organizations can remain ahead of the curve by adopting AI-assisted, layered identity verification strategies which validate users at each stage of their customer journey.
